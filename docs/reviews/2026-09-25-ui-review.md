# BetterScreenshot UI review, 2026-09-25

An independent review of how BetterScreenshot's user interface looks and behaves. It covers the app as it stands on
`main` at commit `149f89f`, after the "v3" editor and recording overhaul. Every score comes from screenshots taken for
this review. The existing snapshots in `docs/parity-v3/` and the builders' notes were not trusted. How the screenshots
were made, and what could not be captured, is in section 5.

**Terms used in this report**
- **HUD look:** the dark, translucent, rounded panel style the v3 spec (§2) asks every surface to share: a blurred
  background (`NSVisualEffectView`, material `.hudWindow`), a 1px border at 10% white, and 12pt corners.
- **Inspector:** the right-hand panel in the annotation editor that holds the options for the current tool or selection.
- **Hint line:** the one-sentence explanation shown at the bottom of the editor, record strip and video editor.
- **Scrim:** a semi-transparent tint painted behind the Quick Access card's buttons so they stay readable over any image.
- **SF Symbols:** Apple's built-in icon set, used for every icon in the app.
- **Masonry:** the Settings window's three-column card layout.
- **Contrast ratio:** the WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines) measure of how readable text is against its background. 4.5:1 is the usual minimum for
  small text; 3:1 is hard to read; below 2:1 is practically invisible.
- **Severity:**
  - **High:** broken, unreadable or misleading in normal use.
  - **Medium:** clearly visible, and it hurts clarity or polish.
  - **Low:** polish or nitpick.

**Scoring scale (the owner's)**
- **8–10:** basically perfect. 10 is reserved for CleanShot X / Apple quality.
- **4–7:** a great app with a nice UI, but with noticeable issues.
- **1–3:** not up to standard: big UI issues.

---

## 1. Summary

**Overall: 5 / 10.** Most of the v3 work is good.
- The record strip, the live recording pill, the video editor and the Settings window are labelled, grouped, explained
  and well spaced (7, 7, 7 and 6).
- The editor's inspector is a big step up from the old single-row bar.

Two defects pull the overall score down. Both show up in everyday use, and both are cheap to fix:
- **The Quick Access card.** It is the thing users see after every capture. An extra image view that `NSImageView`
  adds on its own is drawn on top of the card's picture and its contrast tint. So the tint almost never shows, wide and
  tall captures show two overlapping copies of the image, and on busy screenshots the buttons disappear.
- **The annotation editor's title bar.** The Undo, Redo and "hide inspector" buttons are laid out in a zero-width
  container, so they are invisible.

Other problems add up:
- The editor's dark panels turn washed-out grey in light mode.
- Every ⓘ help popover in Settings is cut off after one line.
- The countdown number is not centred in its box.
- The "one visual language" goal isn't met yet: the HUD surfaces use three different greys.

Fixing the first five items in the top-10 list (section 4) is a few hours of work. By my estimate that would lift the
app to about a 7.

| Surface | Score | One-line verdict |
|---|---|---|
| ★ Annotation editor | **5** | A well-organised, labelled dark inspector, but the title-bar Undo/Redo/panel buttons are invisible, the panels wash out in light mode, and turning on Outline makes white text illegible. |
| ★ Record setup strip | **7** | Clear icon + caption + dropdown groups and a useful hint line. It goes washed-out grey over light windows and leaves an unexplained empty band. |
| ★ Live recording pill | **7** | Labelled toggles, a strong red mute state, good collapsed form. Its width jumps on confirm, and "Paused" and the icon-only actions rely on tooltips. |

| ★ Settings window | **6** | Handsome monochrome cards with an ⓘ on every row, but every ⓘ popover cuts its explanation to one line, and the shortcuts help text names a button that doesn't exist. |
| Quick Access card | **3** | A hidden layer bug: letterbox bands, doubled images on wide/tall captures, and buttons illegible on busy images. |
| History window | **6** | Clean grid and clear selection. The action bar truncates at minimum width, cells carry no names, sizes or durations, and the empty state is bare. |
| Welcome / onboarding | **6** | Short and friendly. The shortcut list is ragged, the modifier order is wrong, and the shortcuts are hard-coded. |
| Overlays & small HUDs (countdown, area selection, window picker, toasts, pin, menu) | **5** | Functional, but the countdown digit is off-centre, the selection size label is unreadable on light content, and the pin's close button vanishes on light images. |

**What works**
- **Toolbar.** Tools are grouped with separators (shapes | text / counter / highlighter | redaction / spotlight | crop).
  The selected tool is an accent-filled square and hover shows a subtle fill (`editor-01`, toolbar). Tooltips name the
  tool and its single-key shortcut ("Arrow (A)").
- **Panel layout.** The inspector heading names the tool or selection ("Arrow", "Text", "4 objects", "Nothing
  selected"). Sections have small uppercase captions and hairline dividers on 16pt insets. I found nothing overlapping,
  and every label and segment fits at the 232pt column width (automated check).
- **Explanations everywhere.** The hint line changes with context: "Drag to draw an arrow — it points to where you let
  go." / "Type your text — ↩ or Esc finishes it, ⇧↩ starts a new line." / "Drag to move it, drag a corner to resize
  the text…" (`editor-13`). Redaction modes explain themselves ("Covers it with solid black — the safest choice…").
  Crop and Select show a plain note.
- **Style chips.** The Text Styles chips (Label, Callout, Note, Code, Title, Subtle) preview their own look, and the
  active one gets a blue ring.
- **Controls.** Sliders show their value with a unit ("12 px", "60%", "40%"), and Thin/Medium/Thick give quick
  presets.
- **Rendering and zoom.** Annotations render crisply (`editor-06`: arrow, box, numbered counters, highlighter, blur,
  label). Zooming to 150% keeps the chrome stable, and the zoom control reads "Fit · 115%" / "150%".

**What works**

  cleanly.
- Hover gives a soft fill on each button.
- Recordings get the right set of actions: Copy file, Edit video, Open, Show in Finder, Close.

**Issues**

| # | Sev. | Where | Problem and why it matters | Suggested fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | **High** | Every card | **The card draws its image twice, and the contrast tint is hidden.** `DraggableImageView` is an `NSImageView`, and on this macOS (26 / Darwin 25.6) `NSImageView` adds its own internal view with a *fit-inside* copy of the image. That copy is drawn **on top of** the code's *fill-the-card* copy and on top of the tint behind the buttons. Layer dump: tint layer at y 0–67pt, `NSImageView`'s own image layer at y 9–140pt above it. What users see: **(a)** the tint only shows in the thin top and bottom bands, so on busy images the buttons are illegible (the "SUMMER SALE" card, and the stripes test above). ## 3. Cross-cutting issues

- **C1. There is no single HUD style yet (spec §2 goal).** I measured four "dark HUD" backgrounds:
  - editor panels ≈ #222, blending within the window over a dark backdrop;
  - the pill ≈ #5f5f5f, blurred with a 25% black tint;
  - the strip, countdown and toasts ≈ #7d7e7e over light content, blurred with no tint;
  - editor panels in light mode ≈ #939393.

  Fix: one shared helper (material + a fixed dark tint of about 35–45% black + the 10% white border) that every
  surface uses.
- **C2. Two control styles.** Settings uses custom monochrome controls (white toggles, chevron dropdowns, black cards);
  everything else uses standard AppKit controls. Either is fine, but the *same setting* looks different in Settings
  and in the strip.
- **C3. Terminology drift.** Pick one term per concept:
  - "System audio" (strip, Settings) vs "Sound" (pill)
  - "Camera size" vs "Bubble Size"
  - "Show mouse cursor" vs "Cursor: Visible"
  - "Full Screen" (strip) vs "Fullscreen" (menu, Settings: "Capture Fullscreen")
  - "Mute segment" vs "Mute Segment"
  - "Edit Video…" (History) vs "Edit video" (card tooltip)
  - ⌘⇧ (onboarding) vs ⇧⌘ (everywhere else)
- **C4. Icon-only buttons rely on tooltips.** The editor and strip back icons up with a hint line. The Quick Access
  card, the pill's actions and the video editor's undo/redo don't. That's acceptable for standard glyphs, but the spec
  says "no unexplained icons".
- **C5. Light mode.** Settings, strip, pill, video editor and countdown are forced dark, which is consistent. History
  and onboarding adapt properly. The annotation editor half-adapts (light window, washed-out dark panels) and is the
  one surface that breaks in light mode (E2).
- **C6. Typography and spacing.** The system font is used throughout, apart from the editor's monospaced size label.
  The small uppercase section captions are shared by the editor and Settings, which is good. The 8pt grid and 16pt
  insets hold in the editor panel and Settings. The strip's vertical rhythm is broken by the empty meter band (S2).
- **C7. Colour.** The accent colour marks selection consistently: editor tool, video speed/segment, History cells,
  shortcut recording. Red is used only for recording and mute states. Good.

## 4. Top 10 fixes (ranked by impact ÷ effort)

1. **Quick Access: stop `NSImageView` drawing its own copy of the image (Q1).** Minutes of work. It restores
   edge-to-edge images, removes the doubled images and makes the button tint work again, on every capture.
2. **Editor: give the title-bar accessory a real width (E1).** One line. Undo, Redo and the inspector toggle reappear.
3. **Settings: make the ⓘ popovers wrap (T1).** Two modifiers. The whole help system becomes readable.
4. **Countdown: centre the number (X1).** A few lines.
5. **One dark HUD style (C1, E2, S1, X5).** Add the pill's dark tint to the strip, countdown and toasts, and force the
   editor window dark (or give its panels an opaque dark fill). This fixes light-mode legibility and delivers spec §2's
   "one visual language".
6. **Outline colour follows the text colour (E3).** Small. It removes an unreadable result from the text tool.
7. **Area-selection size label on a dark chip (X2).** Small. A readable size readout on any background.
8. **Cap "Fit" at the capture's real size, and size the editor window in points (E6).** Small. Every screenshot opens
   sharp, and small crops stop looking blurry.
9. **History action bar: no truncation at minimum width (H1).** Small: raise the minimum width, or use icon + label
   buttons that collapse.
10. **Shorten the Text inspector and tidy the handles (E4, E5).** Medium effort: compact Background colour row,
    collapsible sections or an Arrange footer, handles outside the box.

## 5. Method & limits

**How the screenshots were made**
- Headless probe programs (small command-line Swift packages that open the real windows off to the side and
  screenshot them) were built outside the repo, under the session scratchpad at
  `/private/tmp/claude-501/…/scratchpad/agents/ui-review/`. That folder is temporary and may already be gone; to redo
  a capture, rebuild a probe the same way. The probes link the repo's `EditorKit`, `RecordingKit` and `OverlayKit`
  packages by absolute path and create the real UI classes.
- App-target classes were copied fresh from `App/` with minimal stubs: `RecordStripController`,
  `RecordingControlsController`, the Settings views, the History window, onboarding and the menu. The stubs are a
  separate UserDefaults suite, a temporary History folder, a stubbed permission manager, and a forced-show switch for
  `InfoTip`.
- The content is realistic: a synthetic "Account Settings" screenshot (1600×1000, also 360×225 and 700×2400), busy,
  bimodal, very wide and very tall images, and a generated 20-second 1280×800 MP4 with a timestamp in every frame.
- Interaction used synthetic mouse and key events delivered straight to the views, `performClick`, and `insertText`
  for typing.
- Windows were captured with `CGWindowListCreateImage(.optionIncludingWindow, [.boundsIgnoreFraming, .bestResolution])`.
  This shows blur effects and video layers, unlike `cacheDisplay`.
- Environment: MacBook with a 2560×1664 Retina display at 1470×956pt, macOS 26 (Darwin 25.6), system in dark mode.
  Light mode was tested with `NSApp.appearance = .aqua`.
- Two helper agents built the recording and app-window probes and captured their screenshots. I looked at every image
  myself and did all the judging. I re-checked the two biggest findings independently:
  - **E1:** printed the accessory's frame (width 0, needs 106×22pt).
  - **Q1:** my own stripe-image probe plus a layer dump.
- Automated checks: truncated labels, squeezed buttons and segments, controls outside the panel column, and WCAG
  contrast measured from captured pixels.

**What couldn't be captured, or may differ on a real screen**
- The probe apps run in the background, so their windows render *inactive*: grey traffic lights, grey default buttons,
  and no key-window focus rings or text caret. These were not penalised. Some video-editor shots did become active.
- **Tooltips** (OS windows) weren't captured. Hover states were produced by calling the views' hover handlers
  directly. The Settings ⓘ popovers were shown through a forced binding.
- **Blurred surfaces** (strip, pill, countdown, toasts) take their colour from whatever is behind them, so the measured
  greys (S1, C1) depend on what was on screen during the capture. On a dark wallpaper they'll look darker.
- The area-selection overlay and window picker were captured as 900×560 stand-ins, not full-screen. The picker's
  drawing code was copied because its view class is private.
- Not captured:
  - the camera bubble (camera permission is not granted in this environment);
  - the menu-bar status icon;
  - the "Allow microphone access…" link;
  - the Clear History / Delete confirmation dialogs;
  - the History "file missing" badge;
  - the real full-screen overlays.

**For a human to check on the real app, in priority order**
1. Q1: take a screenshot of a busy web page and a very wide one, and look at the Quick Access card.
2. E1: open the editor and look at the top-right of its title bar.
3. T1: hover an ⓘ in Settings.
4. The editor with the Mac in Light mode.
5. The countdown before a recording.
6. How the strip and toasts look over your usual wallpaper.
7. Keyboard focus rings in the strip.
